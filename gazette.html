The Gazette

Where government orders are made public

Regulations and orders take effect when they appear in the Gazette. Parliament sees many of them later. Here is how the Gazette works, and the orders recently laid before the House.

What the Gazette is

The government's official publication, and the first public step for most laws and rules.

The official record

The Gazette of the Democratic Socialist Republic of Sri Lanka is where the government makes its decisions public. Bills, Acts, regulations, orders and official notices all appear in it.

It is printed by the Department of Government Printing, which also posts every issue on its website.

How it is published

An ordinary issue comes out every Friday. Between issues, notices appear in a Gazette Extraordinary, which can be published on any day of the week, weekends included.

Each issue has a number, such as 2502/21, and is listed online with its Sinhala, Tamil and English versions. In mid-October 2026, Extraordinary issues carried notices from the Election Commission, Sri Lanka Customs, the Ministry of Energy and the Land Title Settlement Department.

Why it matters

Orders take effect on publication. A Minister's regulations come into force when they appear in the Gazette, or on a later date they name. Parliament approves them afterwards.

Bills are public before debate. A government bill must be in the Gazette at least seven days before it can go on Parliament's Order Paper. The Elimination of Rabies and Registration of Dogs Bill was published on 7 September 2026 and presented on 24 September.

Acts are printed in it. Act No. 24 of 2026 was certified on 6 October and printed as a supplement to Part II of the Gazette of 9 October.
