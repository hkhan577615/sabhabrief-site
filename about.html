How it works

How Parliament makes law, and how we track it

The path a bill takes, the official sources that record each step, and the rules for what appears on this site.

From bill to Act

Each step is recorded by an official source, named under it.

  1. 1

    Published

    A government bill is published in the Gazette. It can go on the Order Paper seven days later.

    Gazette

  2. 2

    First reading

    The bill is presented and placed on the Order Paper. There is no debate.

    Daily record

  3. 3

    Supreme Court window

    Any citizen can challenge the bill within 14 days. The Court decides whether it needs a simple majority, a two-thirds majority or a referendum, and whether it must change.

    Daily recordHansard

  4. 4

    Second reading

    The main debate, followed by a vote on the bill's principles.

    Daily recordHansard

  5. 5

    Committee stage and third reading

    Clause-by-clause amendments, then the final vote on the amended bill.

    Hansard

  6. 6

    Speaker's certificate

    The Speaker signs the bill. It becomes an Act with a number for the year.

    Daily record

  7. 7

    Act printed

    The Government Printer publishes the final text as a supplement to the Gazette.

    Act

  8. 8

    Orders and regulations

    Ministers make orders and regulations under Acts. They take effect in the Gazette and go to Parliament later. How the Gazette works

What gets in

An item appears only if it does one of these four things.

Laws & rulesChanges a law, an order or a regulation, or moves a bill a stage.
Public moneyApproves spending, borrowing, a tax, a levy or a duty.
OversightHolds an institution to account: committees, audits, inquiries, appointments.
On the recordPuts a fact on the record: figures and answers from ministers.

Official sources only. Every item links to parliament.lk, documents.gov.lk, another gov.lk site or the Supreme Court. News reports are not used.

Neutral by design. No party labels and no adjectives that judge. Government and opposition business is recorded the same way.

Gaps are marked. When an official source has not yet published a detail, such as a vote count or an amount, the item says so and is completed later.

Daily and reference pages. This week, Bills and Gazette update every evening around 10 pm Sri Lanka time. This page changes only when the method changes.

Parliament's terms, in plain words

Order Paper
The agenda for a sitting day.
Hansard
The word-for-word record of a sitting, published later.
Gazette
The government's official publication, issued every Friday.
Gazette Extraordinary
An issue published between the weekly editions, on any day.
Regulation
A rule a Minister makes under an Act. It takes effect in the Gazette and goes to Parliament for approval.
First reading
A bill is presented. No debate yet.
Supreme Court petition
A citizen's challenge to a bill, filed within 14 days of it reaching the Order Paper.
Second reading
The main debate and vote on a bill's principles.
Committee stage
Clause-by-clause changes; for the Budget, ministry by ministry.
Division
A recorded vote. Without one, there is no vote count.
Speaker's certificate
The Speaker signs a passed bill and it becomes an Act.
Supplementary estimate
Extra spending approved on top of the Budget.
